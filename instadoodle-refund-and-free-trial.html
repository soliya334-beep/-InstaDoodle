How to Ask InstaDoodle for a Refund

The vendor’s help centre is at help.instadoodle.com, and support runs on a ticket system. Reviewers on Trustpilot repeatedly praise how quickly support replies, which is encouraging for this step.

  1. Open a ticket in the help centre, using the same email you purchased with.
  2. State clearly that you are requesting a refund under the InstaDoodle money-back guarantee.
  3. Include your order number, the date of purchase, the amount and the plan.

What Might Complicate a Refund

Be aware of these in advance.

  • Conflicting periods. If your order says 14 days and you ask on day 30, you may be told no.
  • Add-ons. Voice packs and other add-ons may have different terms from the main purchase. Ask.
  • Subscriptions. If you bought a monthly InstaDoodle plan, cancel it to avoid further charges, and ask whether previous months are refundable.
  • Heavy use. The vendor says you can keep your InstaDoodle videos after a refund. Confirm whether credits or InstaDoodle add-ons you used affect this.
  • Third-party seller terms. If the processor is a marketplace, its rules about refund periods apply as well.

If something is unclear, ask in writing before you buy, not after.

  5. Keep every message in a folder, with dates.

Refund or Chargeback: Know the Difference

These two words are often confused, and the difference affects how you get your money back.

A refund is a request to the seller, or to the marketplace that processed your order, to return your payment under the guarantee. It is the normal route, and it is usually the fastest and cleanest.

A chargeback is a dispute raised with your bank or card issuer, which asks them to take the money back from the seller. It can be useful when a seller will not respond at all, but it can also cause problems. According to ClickBank’s published guidance, if a customer starts a chargeback and then requests a refund through ClickBank, the refund will fail. Banks also have their own time limits and evidence requirements.

The sensible order is simple: ask for a refund first, give the seller a fair chance to respond, and consider a dispute only if the refund route fails. This is general information and not legal advice.

Details People Often Miss

A few small points trip people up again and again.

  • Time zones: a deadline that ends “on day 14” may end at midnight in the vendor’s time zone, not yours.
  • Email address: refunds are matched to the purchase email, so use that one when you write.
  • Spam folders: replies from support sometimes land in junk mail, so check it.
  • Screenshots: pages change, and a saved image of the original wording is proof of what you saw.
  • Partial refunds: if you bought several items, say clearly which ones you want refunded.

Refunds for US Buyers

If you are in the United States, you have a few more routes than the vendor’s ticket system, and it helps to know them in the right order.

  1. The vendor and the marketplace come first. Ask InstaDoodle support, and use the marketplace’s order-support site if your receipt shows one.
  2. Your card issuer is the next step. US card issuers have dispute processes with time limits, which are often around 60 days from the date of the statement showing the charge. Check your issuer’s rules, and remember that a dispute can complicate a marketplace refund.
  3. Report real problems. If you believe you were misled, you can report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and to your state attorney general’s consumer office, and you can file a complaint with the Better Business Bureau.

This is general information and not legal advice. Keep your receipt, screenshots and messages, because every one of these routes asks for them.
