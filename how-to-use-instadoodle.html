Step 1: Write a Short Script

A whiteboard video is a story told in a sequence of simple pictures. Write the story first.

  • Aim for 60 to 90 seconds for your first video. That is roughly 150 to 220 spoken words.
  • Use short sentences. Every sentence should suggest a picture.
  • Open with the problem the viewer has, then show the solution, then say what to do next.
  • Keep one idea per scene. If a sentence needs two pictures, split it.

Here is a simple structure that works for lessons, product explainers and social clips.

PartPurposeExample for a budgeting video
---------
HookName the problem“Money disappears and you do not know where.”
ProblemMake it real“Small purchases add up to a surprise at month end.”
SolutionShow the idea“Track every spend in three categories.”
ProofOne example“A coffee a day is several hundred a year.”
ActionTell them what to do“Open your bank app and set up categories today.”

Step 2: Break the Script Into Scenes

Now divide the script into scenes, each with one clear picture. A scene usually runs five to ten seconds. For each, write one line saying what the viewer should see.

For the budgeting script, that might be a person looking at an empty wallet, a pile of coins disappearing, a calendar with a question mark, three labelled jars, a coffee cup with a price tag and a phone with a bank app.

This list is your shopping list. In InstaDoodle, you will look for each item in the library first, and use AI only for the ones that are missing.

Step 3: Choose a Template, Background and Hand

Open a new InstaDoodle project and set the aspect ratio. Use 16:9 for YouTube and presentations, 9:16 for stories and shorts, and 1:1 or 4:5 for feeds. The vendor lists all four.

Then pick a starting template if one fits your topic, since 14 or more are listed, and choose a background such as a whiteboard, blackboard or math paper. Pick one drawing hand and keep it for the whole video, because switching hands mid-way looks inconsistent.

Step 4: Build Scenes Library First, AI Second

This is the single biggest tip for getting good results with InstaDoodle.

  1. Search the InstaDoodle library of more than 1,000 doodles for each item on your scene list.
  2. Drag the best matches onto the canvas and arrange them.
  3. For anything missing, write an AI prompt and generate it.
  4. Regenerate if the first result looks wrong. You can ask for new variations of an element.

Why this order? The InstaDoodle library is consistent and costs nothing in credits. The AI is flexible but its quality varies, and the lifetime InstaDoodle tiers list 150 credits. Using the library for most of a scene keeps your look steady and your credits safe.

How to write a good prompt

Prompts work best when they are specific and visual. Describe the subject, the action and the setting, and leave out abstract words.

Weak promptBetter prompt
------
“Success”“A smiling person holding a trophy on a podium”
“Marketing”“A woman pointing at a chart on a whiteboard”
“Stress”“A man at a desk with papers piled high and a sweat drop”
“Healthy food”“A bowl of salad with tomatoes, cucumber and a fork”

A few more habits help. Mention one subject per prompt, since two characters in one prompt are more likely to go wrong. Describe the pose, for example pointing, sitting or waving. Generate one element at a time, then combine them in the editor. If you are making a black-and-white video, say so, and if your InstaDoodle tier supports color, decide on color before you start.

Step 5: Set the Order and Animation

Now decide how each element appears.

  • Use draw for the main subject, so the hand appears to sketch it. This is the signature whiteboard effect.
  • Use wipe or slide for labels and text.
  • Use fade or pop-in for small accents.
  • Add a transition between scenes, such as fade, wipe, push or iris.

Keep your choices consistent. Using five different effects in one scene looks chaotic. Pick two or three effects and reuse them. Remember that elements appear one after another, so the order in which you place them is also the order viewers will see.

Step 6: Add Your Voiceover

Record your narration before final timing, or after the InstaDoodle scenes if you prefer to adjust the pictures to your voice. InstaDoodle lets you upload or record audio, and the lifetime listing mentions automatic audio sync with slides.

Tips for a clean recording:

  • Record in a quiet room with soft furnishings, which reduces echo.
  • Hold the microphone, or a phone, a hand’s width from your mouth.
  • Read the script slowly, and pause between sentences. You can speed things up later but you cannot add calm.
  • Record in short takes, one scene at a time, so mistakes are easy to redo.

AI voices are sold separately. If you choose them, listen to the samples first, and check that your language is supported. One reviewer felt the voice package was weak for its price, so test before committing.

Step 7: Add Music and Check the Timing

Choose gentle background music and set it low, around a quarter of the voice volume. Music should support the story, not compete with it. Then play the video from start to finish, and ask three questions.

  1. Does each picture appear just before or as the narrator mentions it?
  2. Is there any scene that runs too long with nothing new happening?
  3. Does the final scene tell the viewer what to do next?

Step 8: Render and Export

Render in the InstaDoodle cloud, which means the vendor’s servers build the InstaDoodle video while you do something else. The lifetime InstaDoodle tiers list 1080p output.

Be realistic about performance. Trustpilot’s summary says larger projects with many slides can be slow to save and render, and one reviewer said they had to split a longer video into parts. For your first projects, keep videos short. If you InstaDoodle plan long lessons, test a long project early.

When the render is done, download the file and keep a backup. Cloud software depends on the company staying online, so a local copy of your finished videos is sensible.

Common Problems and Fixes

The InstaDoodle AI draws something wrong

Regenerate, simplify the prompt, or use a library element instead. Some reviewers say generation needs several attempts, so InstaDoodle plan for that.

Scenes feel crowded

Remove elements. A whiteboard scene works best with one main subject and two or three supporting pieces.

The video feels slow

Shorten pauses in your narration, cut a scene or speed up your delivery. Viewers forgive a short video far more than a long one.

The InstaDoodle editor lags on a big project

Split the InstaDoodle video into parts and join them later, as some InstaDoodle users do, or reduce the slide count.

The style looks inconsistent

Pick one AI style and one hand and stick with them. Avoid mixing library and AI elements that look very different.

A Worked Example: A Lesson on Fractions

To make the workflow concrete, here is how a 60-second lesson might go in InstaDoodle, from first idea to export.

Script. “Imagine a pizza cut into four equal slices. Each slice is one quarter. If you eat two slices, you have eaten two quarters, which is one half. Fractions simply tell us how many equal parts we have, out of how many in total.”

Scenes. Five scenes: a whole pizza, a pizza cut into four, one slice highlighted, two slices highlighted, and a simple fraction written under the picture.

In InstaDoodle.

1. Start a 16:9 project on a whiteboard background and choose one drawing hand.

2. Search the library for a pizza. If it is not there, write a prompt such as “a whole pizza with cheese, top view” and generate it.

3. Duplicate the InstaDoodle scene for each step, and add simple lines to divide the pizza.

4. Use the draw effect for the pizza and the wipe effect for the written fraction.

5. Record or upload the narration, and let InstaDoodle sync the slides to the audio.

6. Add soft music, render in the cloud and download.

What to notice. How many AI generations did the pizza need? Did the library cover the rest? How long did the render take? Did the result feel clear for a young learner? Those answers will tell you whether InstaDoodle suits the way you teach, and they are the best use of the first week.
