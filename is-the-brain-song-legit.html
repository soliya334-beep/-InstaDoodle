What We Can Verify

Here are the things that are checkable and what we found.

  • It is sold through ClickBank. ClickBank is a long-running online marketplace for digital products. Charges from it typically appear on a bank statement as CLKBANK, which is a useful detail to know in advance so you do not mistake the charge for fraud later.
  • A refund route exists. ClickBank runs an order-support site where buyers look up their order and request a refund during the refund period. We walk through it in our price and refund guide.

None of this proves The Brain Song is a fraud. It means a cautious buyer should rely on the refund guarantee, not on the claims.

Red Flags Worth Watching For

Whether you are looking at The Brain Song or any other health-adjacent download, these signs should slow you down.

  • A fake countdown or “only a few left”. A digital file cannot run out of stock. These tactics exist to rush you.
  • A “regular price” that is never real. If a page shows a crossed-out price of several hundred dollars next to a low price, ask whether anyone ever paid the higher amount.
  • Guaranteed results. Honest sellers say “may help” and “results vary”. Dishonest ones say “guaranteed” or “proven”.
  • Medical promises. Any claim that audio can cure, treat or reverse a disease is a serious warning sign.
  • No clear refund process. You should be able to read exactly how to ask for your money back before you pay.
  • Hidden recurring charges. Check the checkout page for monthly billing or add-on offers. Read every checkbox before you click.
  • Pressure to buy upgrades. After a purchase, extra offers may appear. You do not have to accept any of them.

In this case, the main concern is the strength of the claims rather than the billing process, which is why we encourage a careful test.

Protecting Your Details

Even with a legitimate seller, sensible online habits matter.

  • Check that the checkout address begins with https and shows the padlock symbol.
  • Use a card with strong fraud protection if you have one, or a payment service that offers buyer protection.
  • Do not reuse an important password when creating any account.
  • Be careful with phishing. After you buy, you may receive scam emails pretending to be support. The real order email will contain your order number, and you can always check your order through the processor’s own site instead of clicking links.
  • Watch your statement for the next two months and report anything unfamiliar.
  • Never share your card number by email or chat.

None of this is special to The Brain Song. It is just good practice whenever you buy from a site you have not used before, and it takes only a few minutes.
